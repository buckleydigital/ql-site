The Problem With Solar Leads

Every aggregator sells your
homeowner to four other installers.

The Australian solar lead market is broken by design. Aggregators spend as little as possible to generate an enquiry, then sell it to as many companies as they can. The homeowner gets five calls in twenty minutes. The installer who calls fastest wins - and everyone else loses their money. The incentive was never to send you quality leads. It was to sell you as many as possible.

The Economics

Solar jobs are high-value.
Your lead cost should reflect that.

A standard residential solar installation in Australia sits between $8,000 and $18,000 depending on system size, battery, and installation complexity. Your gross margin on a job like that is real money. Yet most solar companies spend that margin bleeding it back to aggregators - paying $150–$400 per shared lead that converts at less than 10%.

The maths only work when the lead is exclusive, qualified, and fresh. When a homeowner has specifically consented to hear from your business - not a marketplace - your close rate changes fundamentally. You're not competing on price before you've even said hello. You're the only call they're expecting.

The system we build runs continuously. The cost per lead drops the longer it runs as the algorithm optimises, creative improves, and your audience builds. You're not paying per lead - you're paying for infrastructure that generates them, and you own it outright.
