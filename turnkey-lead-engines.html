Against Buying Leads

The price per lead falls.
A purchased lead never does.

Four differences between renting an engine and buying leads one at a time.

1

We only earn if you stay

A business selling leads by the lead earns more the more it sends you, whether or not any of them close. Our fee is flat, so we only earn again next month if this month turned into jobs.

2

Cost per lead falls as you scale

A purchased lead carries a margin on every unit, so 250 leads cost ten times what 25 did. Here the advertising is bought at cost and the fee is fixed, so the $600 spreads further the more the engine produces.

3

You see the account

Ask a lead seller to show you the ad that produced your last enquiry and you will not get an answer. Here you are an admin on the ad account and can open it whenever you like.

4

You approve the ads

Creative is built around your offer and nothing goes live until you have read it. Leads bought by the unit come from whatever converts cheapest, and that is what your first phone call ends up unwinding.

Cost per lead is the number most lead sellers compete on. It is also the one your business runs on least. What matters is what a job costs to acquire, and exclusivity is what moves it: a shared enquiry is worked by three to five businesses, so most of the time you pay full price to lose.
